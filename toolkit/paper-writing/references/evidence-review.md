# Evidence and inference review

Accessed 2026-10-30. Use this protocol to check whether a manuscript says what its evidence supports. It is a reporting and review aid, not a scientific-validity score. Read alongside the [CVPR cases](historical-paper-lessons.md), [NeurIPS/ICLR cases](historical-paper-lessons.md), and [medical guidance](historical-paper-lessons.md). The active empirical writing examples are restricted to the <=2022 corpus. Current clinical reporting guidance below is a separate compliance resource, not a newer paper example.

## Direct guidance from primary sources

The following are paraphrases of the sources' guidance. The operational checks in the next section are this skill's synthesis, not additional venue rules.

|---|---|---|

| [ASA statement on p-values](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00031305.2016.1154108), 2016; [official ASA release containing the six principles](https://www.amstat.org/asa/files/pdfs/P-ValueStatement.pdf) | A p-value describes data compatibility with a specified model under assumptions. | [Nature Communications reproducibility editorial](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-018-06012-8), 2018 | Define what each `n` counts; distinguish independent experimental units from repeated samples; show data spread where possible; explain access to materials, data, code, and source data for figures. | Historical editorial guidance, not a substitute for a journal's current policy. | [TRIPOD+AI](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11019967/), 2024 | For clinical prediction models, distinguish training, tuning, and evaluation; prevent participant overlap in evaluation; report performance estimates with confidence intervals, relevant subgroup results, missingness, model access, and limitations. | Apply its clinical prediction-model scope. ## Derived review procedure

1. **State the claim and its scope.** Extract central claims from the title, abstract, introduction, results, discussion, and conclusion. Record the exact wording/location, claim type, population/task, comparator, conditions, metric, and supporting table, figure, theorem, or artifact. Distinguish empirical observation, mechanistic explanation, formal guarantee, prediction, association, and causal effect. A demonstration on one benchmark does not automatically support a deployment or universal-generalization claim. [NeurIPS checklist](https://neurips.cc/public/guides/PaperChecklist)

2. **Check numeric meaning.** Record metric definition, units, aggregation, evaluation population, and whether higher, lower, or proximity to a target is desirable. For bounded proportions displayed as percentages, distinguish percentage points from relative change and name the denominator. Synthetic example: accuracy from 80% to 84% is **+4 percentage points**, **+5% relative accuracy**, or **20% relative error reduction**. These are different descriptions of the same values. Do not silently replace one with another, average unlike metrics into an unexplained percentage, or infer task benefit from a proxy. Specify AUROC differences on the scale actually used. [ACL metric guidance](https://2021.aclweb.org/calls/reproducibility-checklist/)

3. **Audit the comparison that supports attribution.** For each relevant baseline, record training/pretraining data, split, model capacity, annotation/input access, tuning and selection budget, hardware, inference calls, resolution, and postprocessing. Inputs can include first-frame masks, camera information, prompts, external tools, and reader context. Label numbers copied from publications versus reproduced locally. Unequal conditions may be a useful system comparison, but cannot by themselves isolate the contribution of one component. For a central mechanism claim, inspect the relevant ablation, what changed, what stayed fixed, and whether capacity or compute changed too. Request only evidence needed to resolve the claim, or narrow the claim. The [CVPR cases](historical-paper-lessons.md) illustrate these checks; [ACL](https://2021.aclweb.org/calls/reproducibility-checklist/) motivates explicit run, annotation, and selection reporting.

4. **Identify independent units before interpreting uncertainty.** Separate participants, images, lesions, datasets, training runs, checkpoints, and repeated timing measurements. Ten crops of one image or ten evaluations of one checkpoint do not constitute ten independent trained-model replications. Different training seeds on a fixed split estimate a different source of variability from new test samples or new sites. Cross-validation folds can share training data; do not automatically treat fold scores as independent observations. For paired methods or clustered samples, inspect whether the analysis preserves pairing or dependence. Specify the estimand and unit before selecting a bootstrap or statistical test. [Hurlbert](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2307/1942661/full), [Nature editorial](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-018-06012-8)

5. **Read each uncertainty label literally.** A standard deviation describes spread; a standard error describes uncertainty of an estimator under its assumptions. A confidence interval, credible interval, and prediction interval have different interpretations. Record the interval level, calculation method, resampling unit, number of independent units, and sources of randomness represented. Across-seed standard deviation alone does not estimate variation over unseen hospitals. Check whether the contrast itself has an uncertainty estimate, rather than inferring it from overlap of separate bars. Do not impose a universal seed count or significance test; assess what the central claim requires and report resource-related limits honestly. [NeurIPS uncertainty guidance](https://neurips.cc/public/guides/PaperChecklist), [Nature editorial](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-018-06012-8)

6. **Separate evidence states and inference strength.** `Not measured`, `not supplied`, `not inspected`, `inconclusive`, and `observed adverse/negative result` must remain distinct. No external evaluation supplied means external validity is unresolved; it does not show external failure. Conversely, do not hide a measured failure as merely untested. With a nonsignificant contrast, report the estimate and uncertainty; do not rewrite it as equivalence or no effect. If equivalence is central, inspect the actual margins, rationale, design, and analysis. Observed predictive accuracy or adjusted association does not alone establish that deployment causes better outcomes; examine the identification design and assumptions before accepting causal verbs. [ASA](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00031305.2016.1154108), [Lakens](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/1948550617697177), [clinical design guidance](historical-paper-lessons.md)

7. **Preserve formal conditions.** For every theorem used in the narrative, map assumptions, quantifiers, parameter domains, asymptotic or finite-sample regime, probability statement, proof location, and dependencies. Check that introductory summaries retain decisive conditions. A bound under exchangeability is not automatically valid under arbitrary distribution shift; asymptotic complexity is not measured wall-clock speed. Identify when an algorithmic implementation departs from the proved construction. A proof audit requires inspecting the relevant argument; locating a theorem environment verifies only its presence. [NeurIPS theory guidance](https://neurips.cc/public/guides/PaperChecklist)

8. **Trace reproducibility and reconcile the ending.** Link each central result to the available data/split version, configuration, environment, command, checkpoint, processing, and result file, where these artifacts exist. Record access restrictions and which results were actually rerun. Then compare abstract and conclusion against the ledger: same endpoint, denominator, comparator, effect size, input conditions, uncertainty, and scope. Keep aspirations and proposed future studies clearly labeled. Do not turn retrospective discrimination into demonstrated clinical utility. [ACL](https://2021.aclweb.org/calls/reproducibility-checklist/), [TRIPOD+AI](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11019967/)

## Original synthetic claim-evidence matrix

All examples and values below are invented for training; they are not literature findings.

| Claim / location | Evidence supplied | Assessment | Repair |
|---|---|---|---|
| Abstract: "Accuracy improves by 4%." | Table 1: 80% baseline, 84% proposed | Ambiguous numeric meaning | Write "+4 percentage points on the named test split"; add relative change only if useful. |
| Introduction: "Module M causes the gain." | Full system beats baseline; training data and inference calls also increase | Mechanism attribution unresolved | Report the measured system contrast; inspect a matched ablation if the component attribution remains central. |
| Results: "The methods are equivalent." | Difference 0.2 points; 95% CI [-1.4, 1.8]; p=0.80; no equivalence margins | Nonsignificance does not support equivalence | Describe the inconclusive contrast. If equivalence is essential, supply its justified bounds and applicable analysis. |
| Conclusion: "Generalizes to new hospitals." | One institution; no external results supplied | `needs_evidence`, not demonstrated failure | Limit to the evaluated institution; preserve the external-validity question as unresolved. |
| Abstract: "Guaranteed coverage under shift." | Theorem assumes exchangeable calibration/test observations | Summary exceeds proved conditions | State the guarantee's assumptions and separate any empirical shifted-data observation. |

## Findings, severity, and closure

Return findings with `id`, severity, exact location, observed evidence, claim affected, reason, proposed action, evidence status, and unresolved uncertainty. Severity is decision-related, not a count of checklist omissions:

- **Blocking:** a central guarantee omits an assumption violated by the claimed setting, or an abstract's central number contradicts the result table. Correct the claim, supply the relevant support, or explicitly leave it unresolved before treating that claim as ready.
- **Major:** a component-superiority claim is confounded by extra labels/compute; uncertainty treats dependent patches as independent patients; a nonsignificant result is called equivalent. Narrow the conclusion or repair the relevant analysis/reporting.
- **Minor:** a noncentral table uses an undefined uncertainty abbreviation or an unclear metric direction. Define it and verify the correction against the actual analysis.

An agent reviewer can assess semantics when it actually inspects the manuscript and supporting evidence, and can close a finding after verifying the revision. Record what was inspected; do not imply that all code, proofs, or supplements were audited. Regex can locate candidate wording, references, and number mismatches; it cannot verify scientific truth, causal identification, fair comparisons, independence, or proof validity. No mandatory human signoff, automatic paper-quality score, or unrelated extra experiments are introduced by this protocol.
