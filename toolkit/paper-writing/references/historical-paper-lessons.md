# Writing lessons from papers published by 2022

Use this guide to ask sharper questions about a draft, not to imitate published sentences. The active collection has 300 records from selected flagship venues; acceptance, citation count, and venue membership are not measurements of writing quality. These cases illustrate decisions worth inspecting, including choices that deserve stronger qualification.

## Evidence and access

The examples below were checked against the historical corpus records: publication year is at most 2022 and venue membership is within the collection's allowlist. CV and medical observations are synthesized from the specialists' explicitly bounded reading in `cv-inspection.md` and `medical-inspection.md`; case 8 also draws on `ml-inspection.md`. The synthesis writer additionally read the four ML abstracts reproduced in the collected metadata. This is not a claim to have read 300 papers, complete methods, supplements, or every downloaded body. Extracted text can contain layout artifacts; inspect the original passage before making a consequential edit or evaluating a citation's support.

## Twelve concrete cases

1. **Separate the signal from the system.** In the CV specialist's abstract/introduction reading, semantic gaze augmentation and the complete saliency framework have different roles. Keep SAGE and SAGE-Net distinct in the concept registry. A result for 49 of 56 evaluated cases retains its denominator and case definition; it does not become a general driving-safety estimate. Apply this check when a paper names both a representation and a model. Source: `cv-cvpr-2020-eb08fdd9de58`, [CVPR 2020, Guided Semantic-Gaze](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/html/Pal_Looking_at_the_Right_Stuff_-_Guided_Semantic-Gaze_for_Autonomous_CVPR_2020_paper.html).

2. **Explain the task through a discriminating example.** The CV specialist observed that individual motion and group activity are made distinguishable through the volleyball setting. Static pose information and dynamic video information retain identifiable roles. Ask a reader to explain the target prediction before introducing its module names. An example is useful only if it teaches a distinction the method actually uses. Source: `cv-cvpr-2020-0f3382aa49ec`, [CVPR 2020, Actor-Transformers](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/html/Gavrilyuk_Actor-Transformers_for_Group_Activity_Recognition_CVPR_2020_paper.html).

3. **Keep the evaluated subset attached to the improvement.** The CV specialist found that the mapping paper's result concerns a particular object subset and benchmark context. Editing for brevity must retain the relevant category boundary, comparator, metric, and relative/absolute interpretation. If an abstract cannot accommodate every result, select a central result rather than broadening its population. Source: `cv-cvpr-2022-714b67dc64b1`, [CVPR 2022, Adaptive Object Graphs](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/html/Saha_The_Pedestrian_Next_to_the_Lamppost_Adaptive_Object_Graphs_for_CVPR_2022_paper.html).

4. **Resolve apparent contradictions through assumptions.** The CV specialist distinguished an estimator's optimality under fixed correspondences from learning better correspondences. An extra training operation also differs from deployed inference cost. Before replacing an apparent contradiction with softer language, compare the optimized variable, fixed assumptions, and operating phase. This check is especially useful when an introduction challenges a well-known optimum. Source: `cv-iccv-2021-467dec39d937`, [ICCV 2021, A Spoonful of Refinements](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2021/html/Agostinho_Just_A_Spoonful_of_Refinements_Helps_the_Registration_Error_Go_ICCV_2021_paper.html).

5. **Treat a theorem's domain as part of its claim.** The synthesis writer read the abstract's distinction between permutation-equivariant functions and the setting with positional encoding. Continuity and compact-domain qualifications constrain what the approximation result means. A shorter sentence saying that Transformers approximate any function would lose the actual theorem scope. Verify exact hypotheses against the theorem before editing beyond the abstract. Source: `ml-iclr-2020-03efb308deda`, [ICLR 2020, Transformer universal approximation](https://iclr.cc/virtual_2020/poster_ByxRM0Ntvr.html).

6. **Keep comparator-specific gains separate.** The synthesis writer read two different comparisons in the Unbiased Teacher abstract: a gain over a competing method under one labeled-data setting and gains over a supervised baseline under other settings. Put comparator and label fraction in the result ledger, alongside mAP and the absolute scale. A matching dataset name does not make two experimental conditions interchangeable. Source: `ml-iclr-2021-00910ff7ae1f`, [ICLR 2021, Unbiased Teacher](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2021/poster/2757).

7. **Name the modeling assumption before its intended effect.** The synthesis writer read the uncertainty paper's progression from feature statistics to a probabilistic hypothesis and synthesized training statistics. Preserve the distinction between a hypothesized distribution and a demonstrated distribution of all real domain shifts. Ask which intervention is performed during training and which robustness outcome is measured at evaluation. The abstract alone does not establish all those experiments' validity. Source: `ml-iclr-2022-00f0b130e168`, [ICLR 2022, Uncertainty Modeling](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2022/poster/7084).

8. **Connect explanation to a testable intervention.** The synthesis writer read how normalization placement, initialization gradients, learning-rate warm-up, and empirical training behavior are linked in the abstract. The ML specialist's abstract/introduction reading adds two useful boundaries: initialization analysis does not prove the entire optimization trajectory, and comparable accuracy with less tuning differs from higher accuracy. Preserve Pre-LN/Post-LN definitions and the initialization condition. A theoretical explanation motivates the change; measured training behavior supports the practical result. Neither should silently replace the other. Source: `ml-icml-2020-00a26fc9c950`, [ICML 2020, Layer Normalization](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/xiong20b.html).

9. **Preserve the statistical question.** The medical specialist read the amoxicillin abstract and selected introduction/methods/results prefixes. A duration comparison framed as noninferiority needs its margin and analysis; a nonsignificant superiority test is insufficient. Keep randomized and observed-outcome populations separate. Apply a design-specific reporting checklist when the manuscript makes a corresponding inference. Source: `med-32609979`, [NEJM 2020, Amoxicillin duration](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/32609979).

10. **Keep endpoint hierarchy and units distinct.** In the medical specialist's selected EMBED passages, patient outcomes and physician adoption represent different questions. Clusters, departments, physicians, visits, and unique patients also denote different units. A positive adoption result cannot substitute for the primary patient result. Use an endpoint map when related outcomes invite an attractive but unsupported narrative. Source: `med-35760423`, [BMJ 2022, EMBED trial](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/35760423).

11. **Carry analytical disagreement into the interpretation.** The medical specialist observed attenuation under the Scotland vaccine paper's self-controlled analysis. Preserve differences between designs, relative and absolute estimates, and residual-confounding qualifications. A discussion can explain why estimates differ, but should identify an explanation as an interpretation unless the relevant analysis supports it. Source: `med-34108714`, [Nature Medicine 2021, Vaccines and vascular events](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/34108714).

12. **Separate biological mechanism from clinical benefit.** The medical specialist's selected cGAS-STING passages involve human tissue, an engineered lung model, and mice. Give each evidence item its biological system. An intervention in mice can support a mechanistic argument and motivate a clinical hypothesis without establishing benefit in patients. Do not force this article's section arrangement into a universal IMRAD template. Source: `med-35045565`, [Nature 2022, cGAS-STING](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/35045565).

## Use the cases on a manuscript

Before polishing, write a one-sentence question and a separate answer supported by the supplied evidence. Build a ledger of problem, limitation, mechanism, evaluation, and boundary. If the mechanism does not address the stated limitation, repair the argument or narrow the claim; clearer adjectives cannot bridge that gap (cases 2, 7, 8).

For each concept, record its canonical term, definition, allowed aliases, symbol, unit, input/output role, and first-use location. Permit useful repetition. Check whether a synonym changes the target task, representation, endpoint, or population. Define necessary jargon close to its first consequential use. Spell out an acronym when it reduces reader effort; expanding every specialist term mechanically can make prose harder to follow (cases 1, 2, 4, 8, 10).

An abstract should let its intended reader recover the question, approach or design, central result, and scope. An introduction should explain why the limitation matters and why the proposed capability addresses it. These are comprehension goals, not mandatory sentence orders. Avoid generic background that delays the actual uncertainty (cases 2, 7–9).

Give a blind reader the abstract/introduction and request a teach-back: What is being predicted, estimated, explained, or proved? What fails? What changes? What evidence would support the answer? What is excluded? Compare the teach-back to the evidence ledger only afterward. A misunderstanding may reveal an ambiguous term, missing premise, or unsupported scientific leap; sentence length alone cannot diagnose it (cases 1–5, 12).

Reconcile repeated quantitative claims using metric, direction, comparator, population, time window, denominator, unit, and uncertainty type. Reconcile conclusions using the same design and scope. In discussion, distinguish observed results, supported interpretations, alternative explanations, and proposed future tests (cases 3, 5, 6, 9–12).

For evaluation, rate claim/evidence alignment, concept stability, explanation, and reader comprehension separately, with a passage and reason for every rating. Mark unavailable evidence unassessed. A mechanical pass, a polished abstract, or a high score does not certify a correct paper or predict acceptance. Consult the skill's scorecard for the scoring procedure and verify current venue rules separately from this historical example collection.
